## What this is, and what it is not

NIGRANI evaluates each sanctioned MPLADS work against a versioned rulebook of ten rules carrying 144 points of weight. A rule it cannot evaluate is recorded as **skipped**, with the reason it could not be evaluated, and its weight is never redistributed to the rules that did run. The share of rulebook weight a case could actually be checked against is its **coverage**.

On the 27,078 sanctioned works in this corpus, mean coverage is **58.5%**. A little under three-fifths of the rulebook can be run on the average published work. This document measures where the other two-fifths went, and what MoSPI would have to publish to get it back.

**Nothing here is an allegation.** Every figure is a count of rules NIGRANI could or could not evaluate. A skipped rule is not a finding against a work, an agency or a member; it is a statement about what the published record does not say.

## Method

For each field, measure what its absence costs the rulebook that exists today, using only facts already recorded in the corpus and in the reasoning trace.

**Measured exactly, from the recorded trace:**

- Which rules skip because the field is absent, counted from the skip reasons the engine recorded, on works meeting a stated condition read straight off the row.
- The rulebook weight those skips leave unrealised, summed over the corpus.
- The number of distinct works carrying at least one such skip.
- The corpus mean coverage as it stands and as it would stand if those skips became evaluable - evaluable, not fired. Both come from the same coverage function the case body uses.

**Extrapolated, and labelled as such wherever it appears:**

- How many additional rule fires there would be, if a newly evaluable rule fired at the rate it fires today among the works where it IS evaluable. The rate is measured over a named population; multiplying it by a measured skip count is an extrapolation a reader can check.

**Refused, deliberately:**

- No value is invented, imputed or simulated for a field MoSPI does not publish, and no work is re-scored as if the field were present. A score derived from a value nobody published would be a fabricated score.
- The extrapolated fire counts are never allocated to specific works, so the severity-band effect is reported as a floor and a ceiling and never as a point estimate. Between the two endpoints the measurement licenses nothing.
- The corroboration bonus is held at its measured value inside the ceiling, because re-resolving it would mean deciding which cases became HIGH.

The last group is the reason this document can be checked. Every number below either counts rows the engine wrote, or multiplies a stated rate by a stated count. None of them is the output of a work re-scored against a value nobody published.

## The finding, in one paragraph

**All 7 of the fields MoSPI does not collect at all cost the current rulebook exactly zero unrealised points.** Not because their absence does not matter, but because NIGRANI contains no rule that reads them: a rule that could never be written cannot be skipped. They tie at zero, and this document reports them as tied rather than inventing an order for them.

The entire **measured** detection loss in NIGRANI today comes from 2 fields that MoSPI **already publishes, and publishes incompletely**. The largest is **complete expenditure linkage**: 70,647 skipped rules across 23,549 works, 1,177,450 points of rulebook weight left unrealised, and a mean coverage over all 27,078 sanctioned works that would rise from 58.5% to 88.9% if that gap were closed.

That is the recommendation this document leads with, and it is the cheapest one on the list: it asks MoSPI to collect nothing new.

### 1. Complete expenditure linkage  ·  rank 1

**Effort:** The lowest on this list, and it is not a publication decision at all. MoSPI already collects and already publishes this field. Closing the gap means exporting it completely rather than to a row limit.

### 3. Revised cost estimate  ·  unranked

*A sanctioned amount can only be read against a second cost figure. The portal publishes a recommended amount and a sanctioned amount and they are the same number on every matched work in the corpus, so there is no second figure and no variance to measure.*

**Publish:** One revised estimate per work, with the date it was approved, wherever a sanction has been revised. Works never revised need publish nothing.

**Effort:** Medium. A revision is already an administrative act at district level; the field exists in the workflow and is not exported.

**Measured: zero points of currently unrealised rulebook weight.**

A rule that does not exist cannot be skipped, so this field's absence costs the current rulebook exactly zero points. What it costs is a rule that cannot be written at all.

## Standing limits on everything above

1. **The corpus is a truncated portal sample**, 27,078 sanctioned works out of the twelve exports committed to this repository, several of which stop at round row limits. No figure in this document is a national total and none may be presented as one.
2. **Every figure must be re-measured after a fresh download.** The rulebook thresholds these counts depend on are calibrated against the distributions in `docs/data/DATA-PROFILE.md`, and a new download invalidates that calibration until the profile is regenerated.
3. **`duplicate_work` is not yet calibrated.** Its similarity threshold fires on 61% of the corpus, which is recorded in the profile and carried on the rule's own trace row. It does not enter the two ranked measurements, but it does appear in the corroborating figures for `work_geocoordinates`, and it should be read with that caveat attached.
4. **The labelled synthetic control is excluded from every figure here.** One work in the corpus is injected, carries `is_synthetic = true`, and exists only because no real MPLADS row can populate the certification rung. It is excluded from every count in this document.
5. **A skipped rule is not a finding.** Where this document says a rule could not be evaluated on a work, it is describing the published record and not the work.

Rulebook `v1.0.0`, sha256 `2e1788d80341b6ac...`. Coverage arithmetic is `engine/score.py`'s own; this document does not carry a second copy of it.
